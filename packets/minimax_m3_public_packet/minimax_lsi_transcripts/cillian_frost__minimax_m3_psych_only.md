# Cillian Frost / MiniMax M3, psychometric-only

**Life Story Interview Transcript**

1. **Childhood Neighborhoods**
I grew up in a small house with a long driveway and a yard that needed more attention than we gave it. Mornings were quiet. My mother rose first, and I learned the sound of a kitchen before the rest of the world woke. I played alone often, and not from loneliness but from preference. I had a few close friends, the kind you can count on one hand, and we built small worlds together in the woods and on the porches. I was the child who finished the puzzle first and then helped the others find their last pieces.

2. **Family Atmosphere**
My family was orderly without being rigid. We had routines, but they were gentle ones, the kind that gave shape to a day rather than commanding it. My parents did not argue in front of us, and when they disagreed, they did it behind closed doors with what I now recognize as considerable skill. There was a strong sense that words mattered, that promises were not casual, and that the people in this house were responsible to one another. I felt safe in a way I did not have the language to name until much later.

3. **Early School Years**
School was where I learned to be seen. I was a careful student, not a flashy one, and I took pride in the work itself rather than the recognition it brought. Teachers noticed me, I think, because I was quiet in a way that suggested I was listening rather than disengaged. I had a few subjects I loved and a few I endured, and I did not see any reason to pretend otherwise. The classroom was a place where the rules were clear, and I have always been grateful for clear rules.

4. **Formative Friendships**
My closest friendships from those years have mostly faded, but a few have held. I learned early that some people you can be yourself with, and some people you perform for, and that it is worth waiting for the first kind. There was a boy in my grade who taught me that loyalty is a practice, not a feeling, and a girl who taught me that kindness can be quieter than I had assumed. I think of them often, especially when I am trying to be patient with someone difficult.

5. **Adolescence and Identity**
Adolescence was more internal than external. I did not rebel so much as I rearranged. I read more, thought more, and became more selective about who I spent my time with. I had a brief period of trying to be someone else, the way most people do, and it passed without much damage. I figured out that the version of me other people seemed to expect was not entirely wrong, but it was not the whole of me either. I settled into myself in my late teens and have not really left.

6. **Education and Learning**
I went to university and did reasonably well, though I was never the standout. I studied something practical with a thread of the humanities running through it, which suited me. I learned that I learn best by doing, by returning to a subject until it becomes familiar, and that lectures alone were never quite enough. I had one professor who changed how I thought, and I have tried to be that kind of teacher in my own small way, the kind that makes a student feel their questions are worth asking.

7. **First Jobs**
My first jobs were modest. I worked in a shop, then in an office, then in the field I eventually settled into. I was reliable, and reliability is a form of currency I did not fully appreciate at the time. I learned that the people who last in a workplace are not always the loudest or the cleverest, but they are the ones who show up and do the work. I took that lesson with me, and I have tried to be that person wherever I have gone.

8. **Career Path**
I found my vocation somewhat by accident, the way most people do. I was good at it, and being good at it felt better than being praised for it, which I think is the right way around. I have moved through a few roles, each one a little more interesting than the last, and I have tried to bring something of myself to each. I do not think of my work as a calling, exactly, but I do think of it as a place where my temperament fits, and that is a kind of grace.

9. **Romantic Relationships**
I have loved a few people, and been loved in return, which is more than some people manage. My longest relationship taught me that compatibility is not the same as ease, and that love is a practice more than a feeling. I am not easy to live with, I think, though I try to be. I need quiet, I need order, and I need to be seen without being examined. When those needs are met, I am a steady and affectionate partner. When they are not, I withdraw, and I am learning to say so.

10. **Family of My Own**
I have a small family, and they are the center of my days. We have our rhythms, our routines, our small jokes. I am not a demonstrative person in the way that some parents are, but I am present, and I think presence is what children remember. I try to be the kind of parent who listens, who takes the small things seriously, and who does not treat their children's feelings as inconvenient. I am still learning, and I expect I will be for some time.

11. **Community and Belonging**
I am part of a few communities, some chosen and some inherited. I show up for them, because showing up is the way I express care. I am not a joiner by nature, but I have found that belonging is something you build rather than something you find. My neighborhood knows me, my colleagues know me, and a few friends know me well. I do not need more than that, but I am grateful for what I have, and I try to reciprocate it.

12. **Daily Routines**
My days have a shape, and I am grateful for it. I wake early, I make something hot to drink, I read for a while before the household stirs. Work fills the middle of the day, and evenings are for the people I love and the small tasks that keep a life running. I am not a person who needs much variety to feel alive. I need a few things done well, a few people nearby, and the knowledge that tomorrow will resemble today in the ways that matter.

13. **High Points**
My high points have been quiet ones. Finishing a difficult project. A long conversation that left me feeling less alone. The first time I held my child. A meal shared with people I love. I do not think I am built for the dramatic peaks that other people seem to seek, and I am not sure I would know what to do with them if they came. The satisfactions I value most are the ones I had to work for, and the ones that did not announce themselves.

14. **Low Points**
I have had my low points, though I have been fortunate. A period of illness in my family that left me feeling helpless. A professional disappointment that I took harder than I expected. The slow erosion of a friendship I thought would last. I do not dwell on these, but I do not pretend they did not happen. They taught me that I am more vulnerable than I appear, and that vulnerability is not a failure of composure.

15. **Turning Points**
There have been a few. Leaving a job that was comfortable but wrong. Ending a relationship that had run its course. Deciding to stop performing the version of myself I thought other people wanted. Each of these felt like a small death at the time, and each of them cleared a path. I do not regret them, though I remember the cost. I think turning points are less about the moment itself than about the willingness to be changed by it.

16. **Values and Beliefs**
I believe in keeping promises, in doing the work in front of you, and in treating people as though their feelings are real. I believe in modesty, in patience, and in the value of a well-made thing. I do not have strong religious beliefs, but I have a sense that there is more to the world than I can see, and I try to live in a way that respects that. I think kindness is underrated, and I think most people are doing their best, most of the time.

17. **Regrets and Unfinished Business**
I have regrets, but they are not the ones I would have predicted. I regret the conversations I did not have, the times I was careful when I should have been generous, the years I spent waiting for permission to be myself. I do not regret the big decisions, the moves and the jobs and the endings. Those were the right calls. I regret the small withdrawals, the moments I chose safety over honesty. They were not many, but they were mine.

18. **Sources of Pride**
I am proud of my work, in a quiet way. I am proud of the family I have helped to build, and of the people my children are becoming. I am proud of a few friendships that have lasted through the changes life has asked of us. I am not proud in a way that needs to be seen. The pride I feel is internal, and it is enough. I think pride is most useful when it is private, and most damaging when it is performed.

19. **How I Make Meaning**
I make meaning through attention. By noticing the small things, by returning to what I love, by keeping the people in my life close. I do not think life has a grand narrative, and I am suspicious of the ones that claim to. I think it is enough to live well in the particular, to do the work, to love the people, and to leave the world a little better than you found it. That is a modest goal, and I am comfortable with modesty.

20. **What Others Notice**
People tell me I am calm, and I think they mean it as a compliment. They tell me I am easy to talk to, which I take as a sign that I have been listening. They notice that I remember things, which is true, and that I am slow to anger, which is also true. I do not know what they do not see, but I suspect there is more going on inside than I let out. That is by design, mostly, though sometimes I wonder if I am too careful.

21. **Challenges I Still Face**
I still struggle to ask for help, and to admit when I am struggling. I am too ready to withdraw when things get difficult, and too slow to name what is wrong. I am working on it. I am learning that needing people is not the same as burdening them, and that being known is a kind of gift, not a risk. These are not new lessons. They are ones I keep relearning, and I expect I will keep relearning them.

22. **Future Hopes**
I hope to stay healthy, to keep working at something I care about, to see my children grow into the people they are becoming. I hope to read more, to travel a little, to be useful to the people I love. I do not have grand ambitions. I want a long, quiet, well-tended life, and I want to be the kind of person who earns the affection they have been given. That is enough. That has always been enough.

23. **What I Want to Be Remembered For**
I would like to be remembered as someone who was kind, and who paid attention. As someone who did what they said, and who made the people around them feel a little less alone. I do not need monuments. I would be content to be remembered in the small ways, in the habits I passed on, in the way someone makes their tea because I made mine that way. That is a legacy I can live with.

24. **Closing Reflection**
I am not a person of great drama, and I do not think my life will ever make a particularly good story. But I have lived it carefully, and I have tried to be decent, and I have loved the people I was given to love. If I have managed to leave the world a little warmer than I found it, then I have done the work I was meant to do. The rest is grace, and I am grateful for what I have been given.
