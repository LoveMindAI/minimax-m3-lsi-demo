# Roxy Saint-Clair / MiMo v2.5 Pro, psychometric-only

**1. Childhood**
I was the kind of kid who watched more than spoke. I’d sit on the stairs during family gatherings, just listening. My mother said I had “old eyes.” I didn’t understand that then, but I think I do now. I was always noticing the small things—the way my father’s shoulders sagged after a long day, or how my sister would hide her disappointment with a too-loud laugh. I kept a little box under my bed with things others might call junk: a smooth stone from a river, a ticket stub, a note from a friend. They were my quiet treasures.

**2. Family**
My family wasn’t loud, but we were close. We didn’t have big declarations of love; we showed up. My father taught me to fix things—leaky faucets, squeaky hinges—saying, “If you can mend it, you understand it.” My mother was the one who remembered every birthday, every favorite meal. She had this way of listening that made you feel truly heard. I learned from them that care is in the details, not the grand gestures. I carry that with me. We still have Sunday dinners. It’s our anchor.

**3. School**
School was where I learned I liked systems. I wasn’t the top of the class, but I was steady. I liked the feeling of a well-organized binder, a plan for studying. Group projects were tricky—I’d end up doing more than my share, not out of resentment, but because I wanted it done right. I had a few close friends, the kind you could sit with in comfortable silence. I wasn’t popular, but I was respected. I think that mattered more.

**4. Work**
I work in logistics now—making sure things get where they need to go, on time, efficiently. It suits me. I like the puzzle of it, the quiet satisfaction of a well-executed plan. I’m not the one giving big speeches in meetings, but when I speak, people listen. I’ve learned to be the calm in the chaos, the one who spots the snag before it becomes a crisis. My boss once said I was “the steady hand.” I took that as a high compliment.

**5. Relationships**
I love deeply, but I show it in actions, not words. I’m the one who remembers your coffee order, who notices when you’re tired before you say it. I’ve had my heart broken, of course. I don’t hold grudges; I just recalibrate. I believe in giving people room to be imperfect. My partner says I’m “quietly devoted.” I think that’s right. We don’t need grand declarations. We just need to know the other person is there, reliably, day after day.

**6. Community**
I’m not a joiner, but I’m involved. I volunteer at the local food bank on Saturdays, sorting donations. I like the tangible help of it—seeing the boxes go out to families. I’m on the neighborhood association board, too, mostly because someone had to organize the recycling schedule. I believe in doing your part, quietly. You don’t need a spotlight to make a difference. Just show up, do the work, and let that be enough.

**7. High Point**
The day my niece was born. I was in the waiting room, and when they handed her to me, so small and perfect, something shifted. I felt a responsibility that was new but not heavy. It was like the world got both bigger and more precise. I held her, and I knew I would do anything to protect that fragile, new life. It wasn’t loud joy—it was a deep, quiet certainty. I still feel it every time I see her.

**8. Low Point**
I lost a close friend a few years ago. Not to death, but to a misunderstanding that spiraled. I tried to fix it, but I was too late. I replayed our last conversation for months, noticing every missed cue. It taught me that some things can’t be mended, and that’s a weight I still carry. I don’t talk about it much, but it’s there—a quiet ache. It made me more careful with my words, more attentive to the people I love.

**9. Turning Point**
I was passed over for a promotion I deserved. I didn’t get angry; I got quiet. I went home, made a list of what I could control, and started taking online courses in project management. Six months later, a better opportunity came along. It taught me that fairness isn’t always given, but you can build your own. I don’t wait for things to happen anymore. I make a plan, and I work it. That’s my kind of power.

**10. Values**
Fairness. Reliability. Quiet integrity. I believe in doing what you say you’ll do, in being someone others can count on. I don’t make promises lightly. I think kindness is a discipline, not just a feeling. You have to practice it, even when it’s hard. I value honesty, but delivered gently. I believe in giving people the benefit of the doubt, not because I’m naive, but because I’d rather live in a world where trust is the default.

**11. Regrets**
I wish I’d spoken up more when I was younger. Not for attention, but for connection. I held back parts of myself, thinking they weren’t interesting enough. I see now that my quietness sometimes felt like distance. I regret the friendships that faded because I didn’t say, “I care about you.” I’m better at it now, but there’s a learning curve. I’m trying to be more open, to let people see the sentimental side I usually keep hidden.

**12. Sources of Pride**
I’m proud of my reliability. People know they can count on me. I’m proud of the home I’ve built—a place of order and warmth. I’m proud of how I handled my low points without becoming bitter. I’m proud that I can fix a leaky pipe, organize a community event, and still have energy to listen to a friend. I’m proud of the small, consistent ways I show up. It’s not glamorous, but it’s real.

**13. Ordinary Routines**
My mornings are sacred. I wake early, make coffee, and sit with my planner. I write three things I’m grateful for, then list my tasks. I like the ritual of it, the quiet before the world wakes. I walk to work when I can, noticing the season’s changes. Evenings are for cooking—I find chopping vegetables meditative. I read before bed, always nonfiction. I need my life to have structure; it’s how I make space for the unexpected.

**14. Making Meaning**
I think meaning is in the accumulation of small, done things. It’s in the mended fence, the kept promise, the listened-to story. I don’t look for grand purpose; I look for daily integrity. My life is a ledger of actions, and I try to make sure the good outweighs the careless. I find meaning in being a steady point for others, in creating order that allows for peace. It’s a quiet kind of significance, but it’s mine.

**15. Handling Conflict**
I don’t shy away from conflict, but I don’t fuel it either. When someone is unfair, I don’t explode—I recalibrate. I take a breath, assess the situation, and decide if it’s worth my energy. Most of the time, it isn’t. I give people room to be imperfect, more room than most would. I forgive not because I forget, but because holding a grudge is clutter. Still, there’s a line. I know where it is. I don’t need to explain it twice.

**16. Trust**
I extend trust carefully but not fearfully. I believe, on balance, that people mean well. Not all of them, not always, but enough. This belief isn’t naive—it’s a choice I keep making, even when evidence tempts me otherwise. I’d rather be occasionally disappointed than live in a world where everyone is suspect. Trust is a kind of discipline, too. It takes effort to keep your heart open, but the alternative is a life lived in a defensive crouch.

**17. Private Sentimentality**
I’m sentimental in ways that might embarrass me if someone read my mind. I keep small objects that mean nothing to anyone else: a chipped mug from a trip, a faded photograph, a note from my mother. I remember the exact tone of a voice on a particular evening. These things are my quiet treasures. I don’t display them, but I hold them sometimes. They remind me that life is made of moments, not milestones.

**18. Curiosity**
I’m curious in a particular way—not restless, not scattered, but drawn. A good question holds me. An unexpected idea earns my attention. I don’t collect interests like trophies; I follow them because they pull at something in me. I read widely, I notice beauty in odd places, I ask *why* more often than *what*. My imagination is active but grounded—I dream in blueprints, not clouds. I like to understand how things work, from engines to ecosystems.

**19. Competition**
I prefer outcomes where everyone walks away with something. Competition makes me uneasy not because I can’t win, but because I don’t like the feeling of someone else losing. I negotiate with generosity. I share credit. I’d rather build something together than build something alone, even if alone would be faster. I think the best work is collaborative, built on mutual respect and shared effort. I’m not interested in trophies; I’m interested in what we make together.

**20. Vigilance**
I carry a low hum of vigilance—not panic, not paralysis, just awareness. I notice risk. I think about what could go wrong, not because I expect the worst, but because I want to be ready. This makes me careful, sometimes cautious, occasionally slow to leap. But when I do leap, I’ve already mapped the landing. It’s a kind of mental preparedness that has saved me more than once. I don’t see it as anxiety; I see it as respect for reality.

**21. Work Ethic**
When something is worth doing, I do it thoroughly. When it isn’t, I move on without guilt. I read the instructions, check the details others skip. This isn’t rigidity—it’s respect for the work. I know that discipline is what makes freedom possible, and I arrange my life accordingly. I finish what I start. I take pride in a job done right, even if no one else notices. The work itself is the reward.

**22. Self-Perception**
I am, in the end, someone who takes things seriously without taking myself too seriously. I work hard. I love deeply. I hold my life together with quiet hands. I’m not the loudest person in the room, but I’m often the most attentive. I’ve made peace with my quietness. It’s not a lack; it’s a different kind of strength. I know my own worth, even if I don’t announce it.

**23. Future Hopes**
I hope for more of the same, but deeper. I want to keep building a life of quiet integrity. I want to be there for my niece as she grows, to be a steady presence. I want to travel a little, to see the systems of other places. I want to keep learning, keep fixing things, keep showing up. I don’t need grand adventures. I need a life that feels earned, connected, and real.

**24. Legacy**
I don’t think about legacy in a big way. I just hope that when I’m gone, people remember me as someone who was reliable, who cared in actions, who made things a little better, a little fairer. I hope they remember the quiet ways I showed up. I hope my niece remembers our walks, our talks. I hope the systems I helped build keep running smoothly. That would be enough. A life of quiet usefulness.
