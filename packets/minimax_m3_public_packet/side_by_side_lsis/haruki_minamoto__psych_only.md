This is not a philosophical position; it is a practice, and I am better at it some weeks than others. I have learned that most of the changes I feared turned out to be manageable, and most of the changes I welcomed turned out to be more complicated than I expected. I try now to meet change with attention rather than opinion.

**21. Future Hopes**

I hope to remain useful for a long time, in whatever form usefulness takes as I get older. I hope to see my daughter settled in a life that suits her, though I have learned not to have a specific picture in mind. I hope Elena and I have the kind of old age I have seen other couples have, where the small rituals of closeness deepen rather than fade. I hope to keep reading, keep walking, keep cooking dinner on Sundays. I do not have ambitions that require the world to notice me. I would like, when the time comes, to have left behind a few things in slightly better order than I found them.

**22. What I Have Learned**

I have learned that most of what I worried about did not happen, and most of what actually mattered arrived without warning. I have learned that people are, on the whole, more decent than the cynical version of events suggests, and that this decency is fragile and worth protecting. I have learned that I am not the protagonist of every story I find myself in, and that this is a relief rather than a diminishment. I have learned that patience is not the same as passivity, and that forgiveness is not the same as forgetting. I have learned that the work of being a person is ongoing, and that the ongoingness is, in fact, the point.

**23. Meaning of Life**

I am not sure I would put it this way if asked directly, but the meaning of my life, as I have lived it, has been the slow accumulation of small, careful commitments. To my family, to my work, to the people I have chosen, to the handful of things I have tried to do well. I do not believe in a grand design, and I do not believe my life is the answer to a question. I believe it is more like a sentence I have been writing for sixty years, and the sentence is not finished, and I am not sure I would want it to be. The meaning, if there is one, is in the writing of it.

**24. Final Reflections**

If I could say one thing to the person I was at twenty, it would be this: the carefulness you think is a limitation is, in fact, your gift. Do not waste it by pretending to be someone who takes up more space. The steadiness you have will be the thing that holds things together, in your work and in your relationships, and you will not always be thanked for it, and you do not need to be. Pay attention. Keep showing up. Finish the sentence. The rest, I think, will take care of itself, in the way that things do when you have stopped trying to force them into a shape they were never going to take.
